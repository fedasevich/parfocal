How a shape on a slide becomes training data

A slide is a giant photo of a thin piece of tissue. People and computers draw shapes on it, like "this is a tumour" or "this is one cell". Before a computer can learn from those shapes, other people check them. This page shows every way a shape gets checked, when the result is ready, and what you can still change and download.

1. Shapes come from four places

Every shape on a slide is called an object. An object can be a big area, like a patch of tumour, which is called a region. It can also be one tiny cell. Every object remembers where it came from.

AI

An AI model run

Someone presses Run a model or Find nuclei. A computer program looks at the slide on a GPU and draws thousands of cells or regions. Each one gets a guess, like "Tumour, 91% sure".

AI

An imported AI file

A researcher ran their own model somewhere else and uploads its answer as a file, such as GeoJSON or CSV. The app turns it into objects the same way.

Person

Drawn by hand

An expert takes the pen or brush and draws the outline around something themselves.

Person

A one click tool

Smart select: the expert clicks once on a cell or area and a helper model proposes an outline. The expert keeps it, fixes it or throws it away.

The AI's own answer is never changed. When someone fixes an AI object, the app keeps a copy with the fix and a link back to the original. So you can always see what the AI said first.

2. Checking always happens inside a task

A task is a job the researcher gives to people: "please look at these slides". There are two kinds, and they check in different ways.

QuestionReview taskAnnotate task
What is it for?To find out if the AI was right, object by object.To make a correct drawing, starting from nothing or from the AI.
Who works on it?Two or more reviewers, each on their own.One or more experts, all on the same drawing.
What do people do?Answer a question about each object, and fix or add shapes when needed.Draw, fix, confirm or delete shapes.
Is there a disagreement queue?Yes, when reviewers disagree.No. Experts see each other's work and agree on one drawing.
Who closes it?The researcher signs off the whole task.The researcher signs off each slide.

3a. In a review task, every reviewer answers about every object

  1. The researcher makes the task. They pick the slides, the AI result to check and the reviewers. They also pick the decision style, which is how the question is asked.
  2. Each reviewer opens a slide. The app shows one object at a time and asks a question. The usual style is "How sure are you that T2 is tumour?" with five buttons from Certainly not to Certainly. The keys 1 to 5 work too.
  3. The answer counts as agree, not sure or disagree. With five buttons, 4 and 5 mean agree, 3 means not sure, and 1 and 2 mean disagree.
  4. While answering, a reviewer can also fix the object: redraw its outline, retype it to another class, or add something the AI missed. Every fix is saved in a history that never gets erased.
  5. When every object is answered, the reviewer presses Submit for that slide.
  6. When every reviewer has submitted, the app compares their answers. If they agree, the object is settled. If not, it goes to the queue.

Other ways to ask

Agree with the AI (Agree, Disagree, Not sure). Yes or no ("Is this tumour?"). Pick the class blind, where the reviewer does not see the AI's guess and chooses the class themselves.

Blind means fair

Reviewers cannot see each other's answers, so nobody copies. Some styles hide the AI's guess too.

Comments

Anyone in the task can start a thread on an object, like a sticky note, and mention a colleague.

3b. When people disagree, the object waits in the queue

The disagreement queue is a waiting list of objects that need one more person to decide. An object goes there for one of four reasons.

What each answer does to the training data

Queue answerWhat happens to the object
AgreeIt stays. It uses the outline picked in the queue, or else the fix of a reviewer who agreed, or else the AI's outline.
DisagreeIt is left out of the training data.
Disagree and choose a classIt stays, with the new class.
Use this outlineThe researcher picks one reviewer's outline, or draws a new one. That outline is copied onto the queue answer.
Left open on purposeNobody decides it. It is left out and counted, and the report names it.

The adjudicator is a referee who was not a reviewer. They see the reviewers only as "Reviewer A" and "Reviewer B", never their names, so they judge the work and not the person. Their answer is added as a third answer and never erases the reviewers' answers.

3c. In an annotate task, experts improve one shared drawing

  1. The researcher makes the task and picks a preset, such as "Annotate by hand" (an empty drawing) or "Check a whole slide run" (start from the AI's shapes).
  2. Experts open the slide and draw with the pen, the brush or Smart select. They can also run a model on part of the slide, and its shapes land on the drawing.
  3. Each AI object has a check state: unchecked, confirmed, edited or deleted. One key confirms. A guided check walks through the objects one by one, least sure first if the researcher wants.
  4. Everyone sees everyone's changes, which arrive calmly when you pause. A switch called blind first can hide each expert's new shapes from the others until that expert is done.
  5. When an expert is happy with a slide, they mark it checked, or "nothing to mark" if it is empty.
  6. When every expert has checked the slide, the researcher signs that slide off.

4. Sign off is the moment it becomes final

Sign off is like the teacher signing a test. After it, the work is locked.

A review task can be signed off when

Every reviewer has submitted every slide, and every object in the queue is decided or left open on purpose. An object still with an adjudicator blocks it.

An annotate slide can be signed off when

Every expert has checked it. If the task's rule says so, every AI object in scope must be checked too. Open comment threads only give a warning.

Prove it is you

Signing needs a sign in from the last 5 minutes. If yours is older, the app asks for your password again. A signature by someone who just walked up to an open laptop cannot happen.

The sign off window shows the counts and offers three files at once: the report as PDF, the decisions as CSV and the evaluation as JSON. Each carries a checksum, a fingerprint that proves the file was not changed.

5. The result is the final layer, built at sign off

The final layer is the clean answer for each slide: one list of objects that people checked, each with its class and outline. It is built in the same moment as the sign off.

When is each result ready?

MomentWhat you get
While people workLive progress: how many objects are decided, how many wait in the queue. Nothing is final yet.
A reviewer submits a slideThat reviewer's answers for that slide are in. They can still be reopened by the researcher.
Sign offThe final layer is built, the report and its three files are ready, and the training dataset can be downloaded.
Freeze a dataset versionA numbered copy that never changes, such as version 1, with a note, the filters used, who froze it and when.

Yes, it can be changed later, and every change leaves a trace

Before sign off

Reviewers can change their answers and fixes. The researcher can ask both reviewers to look again, which reopens their answers with a note instead of deleting them. Undo works on fixes.

After sign off

Only the project owner can still change things, and must write a reason. Each change goes into a change log with who, when and why.

The signed fingerprint stays

The PDF says the checksum belongs to the version as signed, and lists the changes after sign off in a table at the end.

Build it again

After a change, the Dataset page has a button to build the final layer again. Every build is kept, the newest is used.

Old versions never move

A frozen dataset version can never be overwritten. A change means freezing a new version with the next number. Every download checks the fingerprint first.

6. What you can download

Only slides that are signed off go into training files. If none are, the app says "nothing signed off" in words.

FileWhat is insideWho it is for
Training datasetA zip with one QuPath GeoJSON file per slide: every object with its class, colour, ID, origin and who made it as a letter, never a name. A manifest lists the slides, the sizes and a checksum per file.Someone training a model, or opening the shapes in QuPath.
Patches with masksThe slide cut into small square tiles, each with a black and white mask where every pixel says which class it is, plus an index table. You choose the zoom level and tile size. It runs as a job and you get a notice when it is done.Training a model that learns from pixels.
COCOThe same patches with their shapes in the COCO JSON format that many AI tools read.Tools that expect COCO.
Dataset versionA frozen zip with a number, a note, filters such as which classes and origins, and a SHA-256 fingerprint. Patches can be cut from a version, so they match it exactly.Repeating an experiment months later with the same data.
Consensus exportThe agreed answer per object of a review task, with the outline the queue settled.Comparing people with the AI.
Report PDF, CSV and JSONThe evaluation: how often reviewers agreed with the AI, per class, with an appendix naming agreed, disagreed and unsure objects.The researcher, a paper or a supervisor.

Arrows, text notes and rulers are for people, so they never go into training files. They are only counted.
